Billinger. Edward, had already had occasion to appreciate his talents, and put them to use. At the time when he carried on the war in Serie, he maintained a regular correspondence with Billinger who had long been known as an able Engineer, and had, indeed, made some improvements of value in the received system of Portification. After his return to Tubingen, he had frequent conversations with the Professor, on different subjects of administration and appointed him, in 1735, Privy-Comseller. This nomination was not a simple honorary title. Billingers saw himself raised at once to a power almost unlimited. He revisited some times pro motion, which he did not think himself qualified to sustain. In accepting office, his first case was to require all the knowledge necessary to the discharge of its duties. He employed almost two years in assiduous laborto to interact himself thoroughly in the statistics of the country, in considering its political situation, its constitution, its interests, and became, at the end of all this study, one of the most enlightened ministere that his country had yet proved. Billinger was placed in a situation too elevated not to excite jedowny and harred. He left is, and waited to get the ministry; but the court refused to receive his resignation, soon after the tender of which, the Duke died. Billinger experience from his successor of the consideration and all the friendship which he had experienced in the beginning of his career. Received into a confidence without bounds, he had the power to realize, without obstacle, those plans of administration with which the most enlightened particulars had inspired him.
